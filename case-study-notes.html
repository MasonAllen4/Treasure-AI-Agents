Treasure AI - Case Study Prep

Case Study Study Guide

AI-Powered Customer Engagement - broken down step by step for notes

Section 1

What Is This Case Study Actually Asking?

A big retailer has messy customer data across 7 different systems. They want to use AI to improve how they keep customers coming back. Your job is to recommend how Treasure AI helps them do that.

You get 20-25 minutes to present your recommendation, then 25-30 minutes of questions from the panel.

There is no single right answer. They want to see how you think, not whether you memorized the perfect solution.

The most important thing Ben said: Start with the business outcome, not the technology. Don't open with "I'd build an agent that..." - open with "The retailer's problem is..."
Section 2

The Client's Situation - Simple Version

Who is the client? A global specialty retailer. Think something like a large outdoor gear brand or home goods chain.

What data do they have? Customer data across 7 places:

  • Ecommerce transactions - what people bought online
  • Loyalty profiles - points, tier, membership info
  • Customer service interactions - calls, chats, complaints
  • Email and mobile engagement - opens, clicks, responses
  • Product browsing behavior - what they looked at but didn't buy
  • Store purchases - what they bought in physical stores
  • Product catalog and inventory - what's available and where

The problem: They have 10 million customer profiles but the data is a mess. Some customers have duplicate records. No one can see a customer's full history in one place.

What the Head of Customer Experience wants: Better decisions, faster. An AI solution live in 12-16 weeks that can grow into more use cases later.

Write this down: The data problem comes before the AI problem. You can't build a useful agent on top of duplicate, disconnected data. Cleaning and connecting the data is step one - that's what the CDP does.
Section 3

Key Terms You Need to Know

  • CDP Customer Data Platform. A system that pulls all the customer data from different places into one unified profile per customer. Think of it as the single source of truth for who a customer is and what they've done.
  • MVP Minimum Viable Product. The smallest, simplest version of something that still works and delivers value. You're not building everything - just the first useful thing.
  • Identity Resolution The process of figuring out that two records are the same person. If someone bought online with one email and in-store with another, identity resolution connects those into one profile.
  • Churn When a customer stops buying from you or cancels. Churn risk means a customer shows signs they might leave soon.
  • Next Best Action A recommendation for what to do with a specific customer right now. Example: "Send this customer a loyalty reminder" or "Flag this customer for a CSR call."
  • Activation Taking the insight from the CDP and actually doing something with it - sending an email, triggering a journey, updating a segment.
  • Guardrails Rules built into an AI agent to prevent it from doing something wrong. Example: never offer a discount to a customer who wasn't asking for one.
  • Deterministic rule A hard rule that always produces the same result. Example: "If a customer hasn't purchased in 90 days, flag them as at-risk." No AI needed - it's just a rule.
  • Baseline The starting point you measure against. If retention is at 60% before the agent launches, that's your baseline. After launch you compare against it to see if the agent helped.
  • Consent Permission from the customer to use their data. Without this, especially in EMEA, you can't legally use personal data to power AI decisions.
Section 4

The 7 Things You Need to Cover

The case study lists 7 sections you need to address. Here's each one in plain English.

Task 1

Clarify the Problem

Before you recommend anything, what questions would you ask the client? What are you assuming because they didn't tell you? This shows you don't just jump to solutions.

Task 2

Describe the Target Experience

Walk through what actually happens. Who uses it? What kicks it off? What does the AI do? What happens after? Where does a human step in?

Task 3

Data and CDP Requirements

What data does the solution need? How does the messy data problem affect things? What needs to be cleaned or connected first before the AI can work?

Task 4

Design the AI Workflow

Show the steps from start to finish. Where does the AI make a decision? Where is it just a rule? What are the guardrails? When does a human get involved?

Task 5

Measurement and Success

How do you know if it worked? What numbers do you track? How do you set a baseline before launch so you can compare after?

Task 6

12-16 Week Roadmap

Break the work into phases. What happens in week 1? Week 8? Week 16? What does the client need to provide? Who does what?

Task 7

Risks and Open Decisions

What could go wrong? Messy data, consent issues, the AI making bad recommendations, the team not adopting it. What's your plan for each risk?

Section 5

How to Pick Your MVP Use Case

You have to pick one use case to focus on. The case study gives you examples. You don't need to cover all of them. Pick one and go deep.

  • Churn-risk identification and retention journeys - Find customers likely to leave and trigger outreach before they do. Strong choice because it has a clear business metric (retention rate) and connects directly to revenue.
  • Customer service agent assist - Give customer service reps a summary of who they're talking to before they pick up the phone. Strong choice because it solves the "limited visibility" problem the client described.
  • AI-assisted next-best-action recommendations - For each customer, recommend what the business should do next. Broad but powerful.
  • Personalized product or offer recommendations - Surface the right product or promotion for each customer based on their history.
  • AI-generated customer engagement recommendations - Similar to next-best-action but focused on communication timing and channel.
Recommendation: Churn-risk identification is the strongest MVP choice here. The client specifically said they want to "identify customers who may be at risk of disengaging." It maps directly to a business goal, has a measurable outcome (retention rate), and the data to support it - purchase history, browsing, loyalty activity - is already in their systems.
Section 6

The Data Problem - What to Say

The case study specifically mentions duplicate profiles and disconnected data. This is intentional - they want to see if you understand that data quality comes before AI.

  • Step 1 is identity resolution. Before you can build any agent, you need one profile per customer. If John Smith has a loyalty account and an ecommerce account that aren't connected, the agent is working with half the picture.
  • The CDP handles this. Treasure Data's CDP is what ingests all 7 data sources and resolves them into unified profiles. The agents sit on top of that unified data.
  • Data quality affects AI confidence. If a customer's purchase history is incomplete, the churn signal is weaker. You'd want to validate data completeness before trusting the agent's outputs.
  • Consent has to be checked first. Especially for EMEA customers. Before the agent can use behavioral data to make decisions, consent needs to be verified at the profile level.
Section 7

AI vs Rules - Know the Difference

The case study asks you to separate AI decisions from rules-based decisions. This trips people up. Here's the simple version:

  • Use a rule when the answer is always the same. "If a customer hasn't bought in 90 days, flag them." That doesn't need AI - it's just a condition. Rules are faster, cheaper, and easier to audit.
  • Use AI when the answer depends on context. "Given this customer's full history, what's the best message to send them right now?" That requires weighing multiple signals - AI is the right tool.
  • Example from the churn use case: The rule detects the at-risk signal (no purchase in 90 days). The AI decides what to do about it (personalized message, offer, CSR call, or do nothing).
  • Always have a human in the loop for high-stakes decisions. If the AI recommends escalating a customer to a senior CSR, a human should review before the call happens.
Section 8

The 12-16 Week Roadmap - Simple Version

They want to see realistic phases. Not everything at once. Here's a simple way to think about it:

  • Weeks 1-3: Discovery - Meet with stakeholders. Understand the data. Map out what exists where. Confirm the MVP use case. Identify consent gaps. Define what success looks like.
  • Weeks 4-6: Data and CDP Setup - Connect the 7 data sources. Run identity resolution. Build the unified customer profile. Validate data quality. Check consent coverage.
  • Weeks 7-10: Build and Configure - Build the churn risk agent. Configure knowledge bases. Write the prompt and guardrails. Set up the output schema. Build the segment that triggers the journey.
  • Weeks 11-13: Test and Enable - Run shadow mode alongside existing processes. Fix issues. Train the team. Get client sign-off on outputs before going live.
  • Weeks 14-16: Launch and Optimize - Go live with staged rollout. Monitor metrics. Fix problems. Document what worked for the next use case.
Section 9

Likely Questions from the Panel

  • Why did you choose churn risk over the other use cases?
  • How would you handle the duplicate profile problem before the agent goes live?
  • Where does the AI make a decision versus where is it just a rule?
  • What happens if the AI makes a wrong recommendation?
  • How do you measure whether the MVP worked?
  • What's the biggest risk in this engagement and how do you manage it?
  • How does this MVP set up the next use case?
  • What do you need from the client to deliver in 16 weeks?
  • How do you handle consent for customers in EMEA?
  • What would make you slow down or stop the rollout?
Section 10

What to Write Down First

When you sit down to take notes, start with these four things. Everything else builds from them.

  • The client's #1 problem in one sentence. "A retailer with 10 million customers can't see their full history and is losing customers they could have kept."
  • Your chosen MVP in one sentence. "Build a churn risk agent that identifies at-risk customers and triggers a personalized retention journey."
  • Why data comes first. "The agent only works if the profiles are unified and clean - identity resolution and data quality validation happen before anything else."
  • How you prove it worked. "Measure retention rate in a treatment group vs a control group over a full purchase cycle."
Remember: They said there is no single correct answer. They are watching how you think through the problem, how you explain trade-offs, and whether you understand the difference between what the technology can do and what the business actually needs.
